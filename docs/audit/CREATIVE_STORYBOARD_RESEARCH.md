# Creative research: three storyboard probes

Read-only research result, not production specs, new capabilities or rendered examples. The existing technology baselines repeated similar text-card geometry. Lookmap compilation alone can change tokens but cannot replace a card layout with a visual explanation. Scenekit already generates timed SVG diagrams, comparisons, steps and illustrative bar charts from explicit `script[].visual`. `contentos/scenekit/planner.py:validate_visual` requires a source or an illustrative label for a stat. The nearest-neighbor example needs positioned points, not a layered node diagram. For that, a local SVG in an agent-authored HyperFrames composition is a more direct route than a new renderer. Character performance remains manual and is not a reason to add a runtime for the first benchmark.

## 1. Technology: nearest neighbor in a toy map

Open on six dots and an unlabeled query pin; make the audience guess. On “closest,” expand a single radius from the query until it touches the winning point. In a second composition, use a close-up comparing two visibly different distances. Finish on the original view, move the query across the boundary, and show the answer flip. No headline until the viewer has seen the mechanism. Use local SVG circles, lines and a deterministic GSAP cue sequence. Mark it as a two-dimensional illustration, not a real embedding measurement. Acceptance: the chosen point and the reason it wins are apparent with audio and headlines muted.

## 2. Finance: the same gain does not undo the same loss

Open side by side with an illustrative balance of 100. Drop one bar to 50, then restore it by 50 percent to 75 on the same zero-based scale. Cut to a large kinetic `× 2` as the shorter bar returns to 100; reserve kinetic type for that reveal. Use Scenekit's illustrative `stat` and `compare` for chart cards, or one local SVG for bars that visibly retain their baseline across shots. No stock footage or market forecasts. Acceptance: chart lengths use a consistent scale, `100 → 50 → 75` is traceable, and the disclaimer fits mobile safe zones.

## 3. History/documentary: how a lock lifts a boat

Begin with a cross-section silhouette and a boat below an upstream waterline. Match-cut from the drawn gate hinge to a wider chamber cross-section. Raise the chamber water with both gates closed; the boat follows, then the upstream gate opens only after the waterlines meet. Hold on the exit. Use one handmade SVG set and a boat silhouette rather than misleading archival imagery; a restrained cue keeps motion tied to the mechanism. Only add a dated or located historical example after sourcing it. Acceptance: the boat, gates and levels are distinguishable on a phone, and gate order is both visually legible and independently fact-checked.

These concepts differ by visual grammar: spatial demonstration, numeric comparison, and continuous mechanical action. Do not score the proposals as creative output. After the integrity boundary is verified, produce one representative render per niche, review full playback and sampled frames separately from technical QA, then have a critic check concept clarity, timing, hierarchy and caption readability. No independent creative agent was available in this checkpoint.
